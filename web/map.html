Three findings
A mesothelioma cluster in North Tonawanda — not the chemical corridor
Niagara County’s mesothelioma incidence is 2.34× the New York State rate. NYSDOH places 13 reporting regions in a highlighted area, and they cluster over North Tonawanda — a different geography from the Niagara Falls chemical corridor, coinciding with documented asbestos users (Buffalo Pumps, Roblin Steel, Durez).
NYSDOH Environmental Facilities & Cancer Mapping, 2011–2015 · benchmark New York State. Mesothelioma has few causes other than asbestos, which makes this the most causally defensible pattern in the project — but it remains a population-level association, not proof of any individual’s exposure.
Trichloroethene at 180,000 µg/L in groundwater
The highest measured concentration in the network, at Frontier Chemical – Royal Avenue (well MW-88-8A, 2020). Tetrachloroethene reached 120,000 µg/L and vinyl chloride 25,000 µg/L at the same site.
2022 Periodic Review Report, Frontier Chemical Royal Avenue Site. Page-level citation pending (see docs/CITATIONS_TODO.md). These are monitoring-well measurements within a regulated site — not drinking-water supply readings.
Manhattan-Project-era radioactive slag still in surface soil
At the Covanta 15-Acre Site, gamma survey recorded a site-wide peak of 420,000 CPM against a natural background of 5,526–7,522 CPM. The source was a single football-sized piece of slag partly exposed at the ground surface; it was removed for laboratory analysis. Radium-226 in slag samples reached 35 pCi/g.
LaBella Associates, Remedial Investigation Report, Covanta 15-Acre Site (2013), §7.2 (p.44) and Appendix S. The exact position of the 420,000 CPM reading is not stated in the report; it is reported here as a site-wide figure.
What this data can — and cannot — establish
It can show
  • Where contamination is documented in regulatory records
  • What was measured, where, when, and by whom
  • Which sites carry radiological designations, and on what basis
  • Where cancer incidence is elevated relative to New York State
It cannot show
  • Any individual’s exposure or personal risk
  • That a mapped site caused any disease
  • A single synchronised snapshot — each point reflects its own last sampling date. Those span 1948–2025: 141 of 785 points were last sampled in 2020 or later, while 182 were last sampled before 2000. Droplets fade with age so you can see this on the map
  • Completeness — absence of a record is not absence of contamination
This is an active compilation. Source citations for Selected high detections are being filled in systematically and are marked pending where incomplete — a pending marker means the figure is not yet fully sourced, not that it is disputed. Corrections are welcome: use Report an error below.
What this does
Enter an address and this reports the straight-line distance to the nearest documented hazard site and to the nearest sampling point where a contaminant was detected. It is a locator, for general orientation.
What it does not do
It does not assess risk, exposure, or safety, and it cannot tell you anything about your water, soil, air or health. Distance alone does not determine exposure — see the notes with any result.
Your address is sent to the US Census Bureau geocoder to convert it to coordinates. It is not stored by this map and is not sent anywhere else.

Highlighted Areas▾
NYSDOH’s own determination. Using the spatial scan statistic, NYSDOH identified areas with at least 50% more cases than expected, where the ratio was unlikely to be a chance occurrence. Each block group either belongs to a highlighted area or it does not.

Read this as a cluster-level finding — it says a block group sits inside an elevated area, not that this block group’s own rate was individually tested.
Select a cancer to map the block groups belonging to a highlighted area.
Standardized Incidence Ratio▾
Observed ÷ expected cases for each block group. Expected counts are age- and sex-adjusted to New York State rates, so 1.0 = exactly what the state’s demographics predict.

A ratio, not a significance test. A high SIR in a small population can rest on very few cases; treat single block groups with caution.

Well layers▾
Each water droplet is a well or sampling station — a blood-red core (contamination), sized by the number of chemicals detected, with a ring colour showing what it samples (see “Filter by sampling type”). The three toggles choose the data source; legacy wells' popups carry a ⚑ approximate-location note.
Filter by When Last Sampled▾
Droplets fade with age — the fainter a droplet, the longer ago that point was last sampled. Fading shows age, not severity: an older point is an older observation, not a cleaner one. Tick a period to show only those points.
Filter by Sampling Type▾
With nothing ticked, every well shows as a blood-red drop with a grey outline. Tick one or more types — bedrock vs. overburden groundwater, surface water, remediation/recovery — to show only those wells (the rest hide), their outlines taking the type colour. The swatches are the colour key.
