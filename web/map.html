What this map shows. Every documented hazardous-waste and radioactive site in Niagara County, New York — together with the monitoring wells and soil samples that record what was measured, where, and when. Every figure traces back to a named source document, and any sample can be looked up by its own identifier.

How to use it. Click any folder tab on the left to open its panel, then open the drop-down menus inside it to switch layers on over the map — hazard sites, monitoring wells, radiation, chemicals, and cancer incidence. Use the search box above to jump straight to a site, well, or sample by its ID.

Continually updated. This is a living compilation. Roughly 75 GB of source documents are still being processed and vetted; layers and detections are added as each one is verified, so the map grows over time. A figure marked pending is not yet fully sourced — not disputed.

This project was developed with substantial AI assistance under human direction; how AI was used, and who is responsible for what, is set out in the AI disclosure. Corrections are welcome — use Report an error below.
What this does
Enter an address and this reports the straight-line distance to the nearest documented hazard site and to the nearest sampling point where a contaminant was detected. It is a locator, for general orientation.
What it does not do
It does not assess risk, exposure, or safety, and it cannot tell you anything about your water, soil, air or health. Distance alone does not determine exposure — see the notes with any result.
Your address is sent to the US Census Bureau geocoder to convert it to coordinates. It is not stored by this map and is not sent anywhere else.

Highlighted Areas▾
NYSDOH’s own determination. Using the spatial scan statistic, NYSDOH identified areas with at least 50% more cases than expected, where the ratio was unlikely to be a chance occurrence. Each block group either belongs to a highlighted area or it does not.

Read this as a cluster-level finding — it says a block group sits inside an elevated area, not that this block group’s own rate was individually tested.
Select a cancer to map the block groups belonging to a highlighted area.
Standardized Incidence Ratio▾
Observed ÷ expected cases for each block group. Expected counts are age- and sex-adjusted to New York State rates, so 1.0 = exactly what the state’s demographics predict.

A ratio, not a significance test. A high SIR in a small population can rest on very few cases; treat single block groups with caution.
The population level incidence data show geographic patterns and do not establish individual exposure, disease causation, or a causal relationship with mapped sites. Small area estimates may be unstable and suppressed values are not zero.

Well layers▾
Each water droplet is a well or sampling station — a blood-red core (contamination), sized by the number of chemicals detected, with a ring colour showing what it samples (see “Filter by sampling type”). The three toggles choose the data source; legacy wells' popups carry a ⚑ approximate-location note.
Filter by When Last Sampled▾
Droplets fade with age — the fainter a droplet, the longer ago that point was last sampled. Fading shows age, not severity: an older point is an older observation, not a cleaner one. Tick a period to show only those points.
Filter by Sampling Type▾
With nothing ticked, every well shows as a blood-red drop with a grey outline. Tick one or more types — bedrock vs. overburden groundwater, surface water, remediation/recovery — to show only those wells (the rest hide), their outlines taking the type colour. The swatches are the colour key.
Site Specific▾
Per-site views for sites that report data outside the standard chemical dropdown. Each site is its own drop-down; more will be added here.

S-Area's 184 wells report Total Organic Concentration, not named chemicals. Pick a year to colour the wells by the latest TOC reading known by that year.
